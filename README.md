## Overview

Global print production can involve multiple suppliers, production facilities, materials, quality requirements, shipping routes, and approval stages. Managing these activities consistently requires more than obtaining competitive quotes. It requires a repeatable process for supplier qualification, specification control, production monitoring, delivery coordination, and performance review.

This repository provides a practical framework for **global print supplier management**. It organizes supplier selection, quality assurance, international production coordination, and risk management into a consistent operating model that can be adapted to different print projects and business requirements.

The framework is intended to help teams make supplier decisions based on documented requirements and measurable performance rather than price alone.

### Objectives

- Establish a consistent supplier qualification process.
- Compare suppliers using transparent evaluation criteria.
- Reduce preventable specification and approval errors.
- Improve visibility into production milestones and delivery risks.
- Track supplier performance using defined metrics.
- Document corrective actions and recurring quality issues.
- Build a more resilient supplier network across locations and production categories.

## Who This Framework Is For

This framework is relevant to:

- Procurement and sourcing teams.
- Print production and project managers.
- Brand and marketing operations teams.
- Packaging and promotional merchandise buyers.
- Businesses coordinating print production across multiple locations.
- Organizations managing external print vendors, converters, finishing partners, and fulfillment providers.

The framework is adaptable. Not every project requires international sourcing, multiple suppliers, or automated reporting. Apply controls in proportion to project complexity, commercial exposure, and delivery risk.

## Core Principles

Effective supplier management rests on six operating principles.

| Principle | Operational application |
|---|---|
| Documented requirements | Define product specifications, quantities, tolerances, and acceptance criteria before production. |
| Evidence-based selection | Evaluate capability, quality, capacity, communication, cost, and risk using consistent criteria. |
| Controlled approvals | Record who approved proofs, samples, specifications, and production release. |
| Measurable performance | Use documented delivery, quality, responsiveness, and corrective-action metrics. |
| Early risk identification | Identify capacity, material, logistics, and compliance risks before they affect delivery. |
| Continuous improvement | Use performance reviews and root-cause analysis to improve future projects. |

## 1. Supplier Discovery and Qualification

Supplier discovery should begin with the requirements of the project, not with a supplier's marketing claims or quoted unit price.

### Define the production requirement

Prepare a supplier brief containing, where applicable:

- Product or print category.
- Finished dimensions and tolerances.
- Substrate, paper, material, or stock requirements.
- Printing method and finishing requirements.
- Color references and brand standards.
- Order quantity and anticipated repeat volume.
- Packaging, labeling, and palletization requirements.
- Delivery destination and required delivery date.
- Inspection and acceptance criteria.
- Required certifications or regulatory documentation.
- Budget range and commercial terms.

Mark mandatory requirements separately from preferences. A supplier that fails a critical requirement should not automatically qualify because it scores well on price.

### Qualification stages

**Stage 1 — Initial screening**

Confirm that the supplier handles the relevant production category, materials, quantities, finishing processes, and delivery requirements.

**Stage 2 — Capability verification**

Request relevant samples, technical details, equipment or process information, capacity information, and references where appropriate. Verify important claims using suitable evidence.

**Stage 3 — Commercial review**

Compare quotations using equivalent specifications, quantities, delivery terms, payment terms, tooling charges, setup costs, and other applicable expenses.

**Stage 4 — Quality validation**

Review samples or pre-production proofs against documented acceptance criteria. Use a formal sample approval process for projects where errors would have significant consequences.

**Stage 5 — Controlled onboarding**

Record approved capabilities, contacts, commercial terms, required documentation, escalation routes, and review dates.

### Qualification decision

Classify each requirement as:

- **Pass:** The requirement is met with sufficient supporting evidence.
- **Conditional:** A gap exists but can be resolved before production.
- **Fail:** A mandatory requirement is not met.

Conditional approval should include an owner, corrective action, and due date. Mandatory failures should block approval until resolved.

## 2. Supplier Evaluation Scorecard

A weighted scorecard makes supplier comparisons more consistent and easier to explain.

The following model is a **suggested starting point**, not an industry standard. Adjust weights to reflect the product, project risk, and purchasing priorities.

| Evaluation criterion | Suggested weight |
|---|---:|
| Print quality and process capability | 25% |
| Total landed cost | 20% |
| Delivery reliability and capacity | 20% |
| Communication and project coordination | 10% |
| Technical capability and specification compliance | 10% |
| Risk management and business continuity | 10% |
| Documentation and process transparency | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

Score each criterion from 1 to 5:

- **1 — Unsatisfactory:** Evidence indicates a substantial gap.
- **2 — Below expectations:** Material improvement is required.
- **3 — Acceptable:** Meets the defined minimum requirement.
- **4 — Strong:** Exceeds important requirements with supporting evidence.
- **5 — Excellent:** Demonstrates consistently strong performance supported by relevant evidence.

Calculate the weighted result using:

`Weighted points = (criterion score / 5) × criterion weight`

Add the weighted points to produce a score out of 100.

### Suggested interpretation

| Total score | Suggested interpretation |
|---|---|
| 85–100 | Preferred candidate, subject to mandatory checks |
| 70–84.9 | Qualified candidate; address identified gaps |
| 55–69.9 | Conditional candidate; improvement or further validation required |
| Below 55 | Generally unsuitable under this model |

These bands are internal decision aids, not universal benchmarks. A high total score must never override a failed mandatory requirement, an unresolved compliance concern, or an unacceptable production risk.

### Example

A supplier scores 4 out of 5 for quality under a 25% weighting:

`(4 / 5) × 25 = 20 weighted points`

Repeat the calculation for each criterion and sum the results.

**Recommended practice:** Retain the score, supporting evidence, evaluator, assessment date, and outstanding actions. This creates an auditable decision record rather than an unexplained ranking.

## 3. Print Specifications and Quality Control

Quality problems often begin when a requirement is ambiguous, a proof is approved without sufficient review, or production proceeds against an outdated file.

A controlled specification should identify the approved product and the evidence required to accept the finished output.

### Specification control

Document relevant details such as:

- Artwork file name, version, and approval status.
- Finished size and permitted dimensional tolerances.
- Material, stock, weight, or thickness.
- Print process and color requirements.
- Finishing, binding, coating, or assembly.
- Bleed, trim, folds, and other production details.
- Packaging and labeling.
- Inspection method and acceptance criteria.
- Required sample or proof approvals.
- Revision history and approval owner.

Do not rely on an email attachment alone as the authoritative specification when multiple revisions exist. Maintain a clear record of which version is approved for production.

### Quality control checkpoints

**Pre-production**

- Confirm the specification is complete.
- Verify the artwork and file version.
- Review proof or sample requirements.
- Confirm material availability and production feasibility.
- Record formal approval before production release.

**During production**

- Check agreed critical characteristics at the appropriate production stage.
- Record defects, deviations, and corrective actions.
- Confirm that any material change or process adjustment is approved.
- Escalate deviations that could affect the finished product or delivery date.

**Final inspection**

- Verify quantity and product identity.
- Inspect the characteristics specified in the acceptance plan.
- Check finishing, packaging, labeling, and visible defects.
- Record inspection results and disposition.
- Confirm shipment release requirements have been met.

### Defect handling

For a quality issue, record:

1. Purchase order or project reference.
2. Supplier and production location.
3. Product, batch, or lot identification where applicable.
4. Description and evidence of the defect.
5. Quantity affected and severity.
6. Immediate containment action.
7. Root cause, when established.
8. Corrective and preventive actions.
9. Responsible owner and due date.
10. Verification that the corrective action was effective.

Avoid treating a replacement or credit as a complete corrective action when the underlying cause remains unresolved.

## 4. Production Workflow and Approval Gates

A defined workflow makes responsibilities and decision points visible.

| Stage | Required output | Release condition |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Requirement definition | Approved project brief | Scope and acceptance criteria are documented |
| 2. Supplier selection | Evaluation record and quotation comparison | Supplier meets mandatory requirements |
| 3. Specification confirmation | Controlled production specification | Supplier confirms feasibility and requirements |
| 4. Proof or sample review | Recorded approval | Authorized reviewer approves the correct version |
| 5. Production release | Purchase order and release confirmation | Commercial and technical prerequisites are complete |
| 6. Production monitoring | Status updates and inspection records | Material deviations are addressed |
| 7. Final inspection | Quality disposition | Defined acceptance criteria are satisfied |
| 8. Dispatch and delivery | Shipping records and delivery confirmation | Shipping documentation is complete |
| 9. Project closeout | Performance review and issue log | Open issues have assigned owners |

### Change control

Any change affecting artwork, material, quantity, finishing, cost, delivery date, or acceptance criteria should be documented.

A change record should include:

- Description of the proposed change.
- Reason for the change.
- Cost and schedule implications.
- Quality or compliance implications.
- Required approvals.
- Revised specification version.
- Date the change becomes effective.

Production should not proceed on an unapproved change where that change affects a critical requirement.

## 5. International Production and Logistics

International production adds coordination requirements that may not be present in a single-location project. Supplier selection should therefore consider the entire delivery process, not only factory capability.

### Evaluate the complete delivery route

Confirm responsibility for:

- Production completion and release.
- Export packaging and shipping marks.
- Freight booking and transport documentation.
- Customs-related documentation, where applicable.
- Duties, taxes, and other import costs under the agreed commercial terms.
- Destination handling and final-mile delivery.
- Delivery appointments and receiving requirements.
- Exception handling for delays, damage, or missing quantities.

Responsibilities depend on the contract, shipping terms, countries involved, and type of goods. Assign each activity explicitly instead of assuming the supplier or buyer will automatically handle it.

### Compare total landed cost

A useful comparison model is:

`Total landed cost = production + setup/tooling + packaging + freight + insurance + duties/taxes + destination handling + other applicable costs`

Avoid double-counting charges already included in a quotation. Tax treatment and recoverability vary, so finance or customs specialists should validate the relevant assumptions.

### Plan with milestones

Track dates for:

- Specification approval.
- Material readiness.
- Production start.
- Production completion.
- Inspection and release.
- Dispatch.
- Customs or border processing, where applicable.
- Delivery and receiving confirmation.

Use realistic planning buffers based on route, season, product, supplier history, and transport mode. A single estimated delivery date is not a substitute for milestone visibility.

### Exception management

When a delay or disruption occurs, document:

- The affected order and milestone.
- The known cause and what remains uncertain.
- The expected impact on delivery.
- Available recovery options.
- The decision owner.
- The next update time.
- The final resolution and lessons learned.

## 6. Supplier Performance KPIs

Performance indicators should be defined before they are used to compare suppliers. Apply consistent measurement periods, data sources, and inclusion rules.

| KPI | Definition | Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| On-time delivery | Share of eligible orders delivered by the agreed date | On-time orders ÷ eligible orders × 100 |
| First-pass acceptance | Share of inspected output accepted without rework or correction | First-pass accepted units or lots ÷ inspected units or lots × 100 |
| Defect rate | Share of inspected units found defective under the agreed criteria | Defective units ÷ inspected units × 100 |
| Schedule variance | Difference between actual and planned milestone dates | Actual date − planned date |
| Quote response time | Elapsed time between a complete request and a usable quotation | Response timestamp − request timestamp |
| Corrective-action closure | Share of corrective actions closed by their due dates | Actions closed on time ÷ actions due × 100 |
| Cost variance | Difference between actual comparable cost and approved baseline | Actual cost − baseline cost |
| Specification compliance | Share of assessed requirements that meet the approved specification | Requirements passed ÷ requirements assessed × 100 |

### Measurement rules

- Define whether delivery means dispatch, arrival at the destination, or receipt by the customer.
- Separate supplier-caused delays from customer changes and other agreed exclusions.
- Define the inspection unit: individual units, lots, or orders.
- Report the sample size and period alongside percentages.
- Separate critical defects from minor defects where the acceptance plan requires it.
- Avoid comparing suppliers whose product mix, order complexity, or measurement methods differ materially.

### Setting targets

Set targets based on contractual requirements, customer commitments, historical performance, product risk, and the maturity of the operation. Do not adopt arbitrary universal thresholds without validation.

Review trends over time rather than judging suppliers on one isolated order. Where the data volume is low, explicitly note that limitation.

## 7. Risk Management and Business Continuity

Supplier risk management should identify what could disrupt production, delivery, quality, compliance, or commercial continuity—and define what the team will do if it happens.

### Risk register

Maintain a register with these fields:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Risk ID | Unique reference |
| Risk description | What could happen |
| Cause | Known or potential source |
| Likelihood | Defined organizational rating |
| Impact | Effect on quality, cost, delivery, or compliance |
| Risk rating | Result of the chosen scoring method |
| Preventive control | Action to reduce likelihood |
| Contingency | Response if the risk occurs |
| Owner | Person accountable for action |
| Review date | Next assessment date |
| Status | Open, monitoring, mitigated, or closed |

Use a documented scoring scale and consistent definitions. A simple likelihood-by-impact model can support prioritization, but critical compliance or safety risks may require separate mandatory controls.

### Common risk categories

- Supplier capacity constraints.
- Material shortages or substitutions.
- Inconsistent print quality.
- Artwork or specification errors.
- Equipment downtime.
- Transport delays and route disruption.
- Customs or documentation issues.
- Supplier financial or operational instability.
- Data security and confidentiality concerns.
- Regulatory, environmental, or product-compliance gaps.
- Overdependence on a single source.

### Mitigation options

Depending on the project, appropriate measures may include approved alternative suppliers, validated backup materials, earlier material commitments, controlled safety stock, documented recovery plans, additional inspection, or more frequent milestone reporting.

A backup supplier should not be treated as production-ready until its relevant capabilities, specifications, quality requirements, and commercial arrangements have been validated.

## 8. Supplier Communication and Accountability

Communication should produce decisions and records, not simply more messages.

### Recommended operating cadence

**At project launch**

Confirm scope, specifications, contacts, approval owners, milestones, escalation thresholds, and communication channels.

**During active production**

Request milestone updates at agreed intervals. Escalate missing updates or deviations according to their impact, not merely according to the number of messages exchanged.

**At delivery**

Confirm quantities, receiving status, inspection results, and unresolved issues.

**At periodic review**

Assess KPI trends, recurring defects, schedule reliability, responsiveness, and improvement actions.

### Responsibility matrix

For each key activity, identify:

- **Responsible:** Performs the work.
- **Accountable:** Owns the decision or outcome.
- **Consulted:** Provides relevant input.
- **Informed:** Needs status or decision visibility.

A simple responsibility matrix helps prevent missed approvals and unclear ownership across procurement, design, production, quality, logistics, and the customer.

## 9. Technology and Workflow Automation

Technology can improve visibility and reduce manual coordination, but automation is only reliable when the underlying data and processes are consistent.

### Useful capabilities

- Centralized supplier and capability records.
- Version-controlled specifications and artwork.
- Structured quotation comparisons.
- Purchase order and milestone tracking.
- Inspection and nonconformance records.
- KPI dashboards and trend reporting.
- Alerts for missed milestones or overdue actions.
- Searchable supplier communication and approval history.

### Automation principles

1. Establish standardized supplier, order, and product identifiers.
2. Define required fields and data ownership.
3. Automate repeatable tasks with stable inputs.
4. Route exceptions to a named owner.
5. Maintain an audit trail for approvals and changes.
6. Review automated calculations against source records.
7. Protect confidential artwork, pricing, and supplier information.

Automation should support documented operating rules. It should not silently approve quality deviations, bypass mandatory reviews, or make high-impact supplier decisions without appropriate controls.

## Implementation Roadmap

### Phase 1 — Establish the baseline

- Inventory active suppliers and production categories.
- Document existing specifications and approval practices.
- Identify recurring quality, cost, and delivery issues.
- Establish baseline KPI definitions and data sources.

**Deliverable:** Supplier inventory and current-state assessment.

### Phase 2 — Standardize supplier qualification

- Introduce a common supplier brief.
- Apply mandatory qualification requirements.
- Use a weighted evaluation scorecard.
- Record evidence, exceptions, and approval decisions.

**Deliverable:** Supplier evaluation and approval process.

### Phase 3 — Introduce production controls

- Standardize production specifications.
- Define proof, sample, and release approvals.
- Introduce change control and defect records.
- Establish escalation rules for material deviations.

**Deliverable:** Controlled production workflow.

### Phase 4 — Measure performance

- Implement agreed KPI calculations.
- Record data sources and reporting periods.
- Review supplier performance consistently.
- Assign corrective actions with owners and due dates.

**Deliverable:** Supplier performance dashboard or review report.

### Phase 5 — Improve resilience

- Identify critical supplier dependencies.
- Assess continuity and logistics risks.
- Validate alternatives where justified.
- Review results and update the framework.

**Deliverable:** Prioritized risk register and improvement plan.

## Operational Checklists

### Supplier approval checklist

- [ ] Required production capabilities have been verified.
- [ ] Mandatory requirements have been assessed.
- [ ] Commercial terms have been compared on an equivalent basis.
- [ ] Relevant samples, proofs, or references have been reviewed.
- [ ] Required documentation has been collected.
- [ ] Capacity and delivery requirements have been discussed.
- [ ] Quality expectations and acceptance criteria are documented.
- [ ] Communication and escalation contacts are assigned.
- [ ] Risks and conditional approvals are recorded.
- [ ] The supplier approval decision has been documented.

### Production release checklist

- [ ] Correct artwork and specification versions are identified.
- [ ] Quantity, materials, finishing, and packaging are confirmed.
- [ ] Proof or sample approval is recorded where required.
- [ ] Purchase order and commercial terms are confirmed.
- [ ] Production and delivery milestones are agreed.
- [ ] Inspection criteria and release responsibilities are clear.
- [ ] Changes and outstanding issues are resolved or formally accepted.
- [ ] Authorized production release is recorded.

### Project closeout checklist

- [ ] Delivered quantity and receipt status are confirmed.
- [ ] Required inspection results are recorded.
- [ ] Defects and corrective actions are documented.
- [ ] Actual cost and delivery performance are reviewed.
- [ ] Supplier KPIs are updated using the defined method.
- [ ] Lessons learned are captured.
- [ ] Open actions have owners and due dates.
- [ ] Relevant records are retained under the organization's policy.
